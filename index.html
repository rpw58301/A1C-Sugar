Why A1CSugar.com Exists

My 6.1% A1C Wake-Up Call & Protecting Your Kidneys Early

When a routine blood panel came back with an A1C of 6.1%, the advice was vague: "Watch your carbs and we'll recheck in six months." Most health websites buried simple answers behind 3,000-word SEO articles, pop-up ads, and mandatory account signups.

A1CSugar.com was built as the exact opposite: a fast, private, zero-login utility where you can type a number, hit Enter, and immediately see what your meter reading means—and how your daily habits are trending before your next lab draw.

The Blood Sugar + Blood Pressure + Kidney Connection

Even in the prediabetes range (5.7%–6.4%), repeated post-meal glucose spikes combined with elevated blood pressure put extra filtration stress on the tiny blood vessels in your kidneys (nephrons). That is why A1CSugar.com tracks both Blood Sugar and Blood Pressure side-by-side—and why asking your doctor for an annual uACR (Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio) test alongside your eGFR is one of the smartest preventative steps you can take.

Important: Take Multiple Blood Sugar Readings Throughout the Day

Blood sugar rises after meals and drops between them. Logging only one reading a day (such as morning fasting alone) hides post-meal spikes and skews your 3-Month Estimated A1C. To see your true daily glucose curve—both peak meal spikes and recovery windows—test at each of these checkpoints:

  • Morning Fasting: Immediately upon waking, before eating or drinking anything (except plain water), to establish your overnight baseline.
  • Before Dinner (Midday Meal): Right before eating to see where your glucose sits heading into the meal.
  • 1–2 Hours After Dinner: Captures your peak midday post-meal blood sugar spike.
  • 4–5 Hours After Dinner: Confirms your blood sugar has returned to baseline before your next meal.
  • Before Supper (Evening Meal): Measures your pre-supper starting point.
  • 1–2 Hours After Supper: Captures your evening post-meal glucose peak.
  • 4–5 Hours After Supper: Verifies how well your body clears evening carbohydrates.
  • Bedtime: Taken right before going to sleep to check overnight safety and stability.
